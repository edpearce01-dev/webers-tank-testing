Drop-Off Prep & Frequently Asked Questions

Should I empty my paintball HPA, CO2, or SCBA tank before dropping it off?

Empty cylinders save bench bleed time, though if you can't safely bleed a tank at home you can bring it in as-is (just never transport or leave an actively leaking cylinder inside the backroom). Every cylinder must be depressurized and de-valved for internal visual inspection and water-jacket testing.

Do I need to remove my paintball regulator or cylinder valve first?

You can leave your valve or paintball HPA regulator attached, or bring the bare bottle if you prefer to pull your own regulator. When we remove a valve or regulator in-shop, it immediately gets a numbered tag tied to your cylinder's serial number so your exact shims, bonnet, and valve return to the same bottle.

Why do carbon-fiber paintball and SCBA tanks retire at 15 years?

Under U.S. DOT Special Permits (such as DOT-SP 14387, 10915, and 10945), carbon-wrapped composite cylinders require hydrostatic testing every 5 years and reach mandatory retirement 15 years from the original manufacture month/year. Aluminum (3AL) and steel (3A/3AA) cylinders do NOT have a 15-year expiration limit as long as they pass visual and hydrostatic expansion tolerances.
